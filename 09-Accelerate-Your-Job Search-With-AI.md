# Accelerate Your Job Search with AI: Study Summary

My notes on the Grow with Google course *Accelerate Your Job Search with AI*. This is an unofficial, independent summary written in my own words. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by Google, and it is not a substitute for the original course.

## Overview

The course teaches how to use AI tools (Gemini, Career Dreamer, NotebookLM, Gemini Live) across the whole job search. It has four modules, and each one adds an item to a **Job Search Portfolio** folder you keep in Google Drive.

| Module | Focus | Main tools |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Uncover your transferable skills | Identify skills, write a career identity statement | Career Dreamer, Gemini |
| 2. Plan your job search | Job search plan, resume, cover letter, online presence | Gemini |
| 3. Manage your applications | Application tracker, job platforms, networking | Gemini in Google Sheets |
| 4. Prepare for interviews | Interview prep, mock interviews, follow-up | NotebookLM, Gemini Live |

**Portfolio by the end:** a career identity statement, a job search plan, a job search tracker, an optimized resume, common interview questions with tailored answers, and a 20-second professional pitch. The course also provides an AI prompt library of the prompts it uses.

## Module 1: Transferable skills and career identity

- Identify skills from past jobs, study, and volunteering that carry over to the role you want.
- A good **career identity statement** is short, names specific skills, and sounds positive and genuine. It is a highlight reel, not a list of every duty.
- Use the statement on LinkedIn, in networking, and in applications.

## Module 2: Job search plan, Resume

**Job search plan**
- Ask Gemini for a plan, then refine it: prioritize by the hours you have per week, ask for industry-specific job boards, and request a checklist or bullet format.
- Gemini can't write to your calendar.

**Resume**
- Use the **X-Y-Z formula**: accomplished **X**, as measured by **Y**, by doing **Z**.
  - Example: "Resolved software and hardware issues, resulting in a 94% first-call positive rate, by providing technical assistance by phone and email."
- Quantify results and start bullets with strong action verbs.
- Look at resumes in your target field and borrow their common language.
- Keep a comprehensive **base resume**, then tailor a version for each application.
- Keep a running log of achievements so updates are easy.

**Working with AI drafts**
- AI is good at keywords, action verbs, and structure, and it lowers the "blank page" barrier.
- It tends to be generic and overly polished. Add your own numbers, goals, and voice.
- Be honest about proficiency levels.
- Treat the output as a starting point, and check it for accuracy.

## Module 3: Tracking applications 
**Tracker (Google Sheets + Gemini)**
- Link the resume and cover letter version used for each application.
- Record dates of calls and interviews, plus offers and rejections.
- Add a follow-up column.
- Add company insights, for example from Gemini Deep Research.

## Module 4: Interview preparation

**Interview types**

| Type | What it tests | How to prepare |
|---|---|---|
| Behavioral | Teamwork, problem-solving, communication | Prepare stories, use STAR with "I" statements, never invent examples |
| Technical | Role-specific knowledge | Review fundamentals, know the tools, explain your process clearly |
| Case study | Analytical thinking | Clarify the facts, structure your reasoning, end with a clear recommendation |
| Panel | A format (group interviewers), not a question type | Research panelists, engage everyone, note names and roles |

**Universal tips:** dress appropriately, be punctual, speak clearly, and send a thank-you email.

**STAR method:** **S**ituation, **T**ask, **A**ction, **R**esult. Quantify the result.

**NotebookLM for prep**
- It works only from the sources you upload (the job posting, company pages, your resume, your identity statement).
- Use it to suggest likely questions and STAR-based answers, generate an FAQ, and create an **Audio Overview** to listen to on the go.
- When reusing a notebook for a new role, deselect irrelevant sources first.

## Key takeaways

1. Treat AI output as a draft. Your voice, facts, and numbers make it work.
2. Quantify achievements (X-Y-Z, STAR results).
3. Tailor every resume and cover letter to the job.
